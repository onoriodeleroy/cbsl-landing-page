~/about

I care less about following trends and more about whether the thing actually works — loads fast, feels obvious to use, and holds up under real traffic.

I'm a web developer who enjoys the whole arc of a project: the early conversations where an idea is still fuzzy, the part where it becomes a real interface, and the last stretch where small refinements make it feel finished.

Most of my work sits at the intersection of design and engineering — close enough to code that nothing gets lost in translation, and close enough to the user that decisions stay grounded in how the product will actually be used.

I like clean systems, honest performance budgets, and interfaces that don't need a tutorial. Outside of client work, I spend a lot of time picking apart how well-built products are put together.

~/approach

How a project actually moves.

Three stages, in this order, every time — it keeps the work honest and the scope under control.

01 / Discover

Discover

I start by understanding the actual problem — the users, the constraints, and what success needs to look like before any design work begins.

02 / Build

Build

Design and development move together, not in sequence — so what gets shipped matches what was actually intended, without surprises.

03 / Refine

Refine

The last pass is where a product goes from working to good — performance, edge cases, and the small details that people notice without saying.
